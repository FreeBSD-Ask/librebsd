## 原文摘录（逐字）

> The BSDi and USL lawsuits will always be remembered as a period of tension in the history of FreeBSD, and they nearly led to the downfall of FreeBSD.

> Only three files had to be removed from Networking Release 2 and changes were made to other files. The University of California added USL copyrights to 70 files.

> The terms of the agreement were secret for many years, but were eventually released under the California Public Records law.

> After deliberating 6 weeks, he denied the injunction and threw out all but two complaints.

> It effectively stalled development on BSD for several years.

> No one wanted to risk getting pulled into the lawsuit. Many of the people who would have contributed to BSD instead contributed code and bug fixes to Linux.

> According to Jordan Huddard (one of the creators of FreeBSD), this episode "effectively ceded the territory to Linux during a critical period in which there was a leadership vacuum and lots of Fear, Uncertainty and Doubt in the BSD community".

> BSDi continued to sell commercial BSD services. In 2000, they were purchased by Walnut Creek (the operators of CDROM.com and parent company for Slackware). The following year, BSDi sold its software business to Wind River Systems, and the remaining company was then renamed to iXsystems.

> There are differing accounts as to whether or, not they got rid of the phone number, but they did fulfil the second part of USL's demand.

> At the pre-trial hearing, BSDi pointed out that they were using code from the University of California, Berkeley, except for six kernel files that had been written by Bill Jolitz to replace copyrighted code.
