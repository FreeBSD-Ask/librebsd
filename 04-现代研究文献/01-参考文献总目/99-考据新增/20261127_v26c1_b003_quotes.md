## ref1575（Twenty Years of Berkeley Unix，McKusick 1999，academic_work）

Q1（block 15，4.1c 与厂商移植准备）：

This system, called 4.1c, was distributed in April 1983; many vendors used this release to prepare for ports of 4.2 to their hardware.

Q2（block 16，厂商选择 4.2BSD 的原因）：

Most of the Unix vendors shipped a 4.2BSD system rather than the commercial System V from AT&T. The reason was that System V had neither networking nor the Berkley Fast filesystem.

Q3（block 25，NetBSD 发行只经网络）：

Until 1998, their distribution was done solely over the Net; no distribution media was available.

Q15（block 25，FreeBSD 组相继成立）：

The FreeBSD group was formed a few months after the NetBSD group with a charter to support just the PC architecture and to go after a larger and less technically advanced group of users, much as Linux had done.

## ref619（The Evolution of FreeBSD Governance，McKusick 2017，academic_work）

Q4（block 9，FreeBSD 命名与源流）：

FreeBSD was named on June 19, 1993 and was derived from Bill Jolitz version of 4.4BSD-Lite for the Intel 386

Q5（block 11，Yahoo 托管与基金会）：

Yahoo ran entirely on FreeBSD and agreed to host CVS and distribution

## ref171（Opening The Source Repository With Anonymous CVS，Cranor／de Raadt，academic_work）

Q6（block 3，1995 年秋启动 OpenBSD 与 CVS 决策）：

In the Fall of 1995 when we started our own open source operating system project called OpenBSD, we decided to use CVS to manage the OpenBSD source tree.

Q6b（block 3，匿名 CVS 机制）：

To resolve this conﬂict we created Anonymous CVS — a mechanism that lets anonymous Internet users access a source repository without compromising its security.
