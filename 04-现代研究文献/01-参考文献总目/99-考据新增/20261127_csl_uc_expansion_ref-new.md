## 逐字摘录（正文）

> UC President Benjamin Ide Wheeler (1899–1919) had been a friend to the Academic Senate for years. He allocated more power to the Academic Senate than ever before; for instance, he created a faculty committee for the allocation of research funds, and an editorial board to oversee the university press.

> In 1919, following the signing of Assembly Bill 626, the University of California incorporated an existing college. Formerly the Los Angeles Branch State Normal School, this teacher's college was changed into the UC Southern Branch. The school quickly gained popularity, and in 1927 the campus moved to a spacious location in Westwood. By 1930, the school had grown to over 6,400 students.

> A 1911 bill sponsored by Senator Lee Gates of Los Angeles called for a new technical school in his southern district, complete with its own board of regents. Political pressure from Berkeley put a stop to this bill. But aware that Los Angeles leaders were beginning to organize a second effort to start a college, UC President Wheeler and the Board of Regents opted to acquire the State Normal School and create a southern campus of the University of California.

> Not everyone was in favor of acquiring this new campus. Berkeley administrators and UC Alumni Association members felt a campus in Los Angeles would compete for limited state funds. Meanwhile, the Los Angeles campus chafed under the rule of a distant university president, and called for equal status as its own, public institution.

> UC President Robert Gordon Sproul settled the matter in the 1930s by articulating his vision of the One University System, which:
> * established UC as the largest single public university in the United States;
> * prevented the emergence of rival public university systems;
> * ensured each campus had the same mission, corporate identity, and policies; and
> * established a single, university-wide budget to prevent single-campus universities from having to compete with each other.
