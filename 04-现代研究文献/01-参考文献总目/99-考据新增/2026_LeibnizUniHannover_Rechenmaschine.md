## 正文摘录（逐字）

The only preserved Leibniz calculating machine ("younger machine"), with eight setting mechanisms, 16 results mechanisms and one totalling device, was constructed beginning in 1690 in Hannover. In the scientific community and in royal circles of the seventeenth century, Leibniz promoted his machine as a "living calculating bench" on which numbers could be calculated automatically. Completed in Helmstedt in 1692 and subsequently improved in the Duchy of Saxe-Zeitz, the machine was only returned to Hannover in the summer of 1716, just before Leibniz's death. The only preserved original machine was formerly owned by the Electorate of Hanover. The device has been acquired by the State of Lower Saxony and is now stored at the Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz Library.

The stepped drum, a component for entering numbers into a calculating machine, is one of the most innovative and lasting technical devices invented by Leibniz. This cylindrical object features nine tooth ribs with different stepped lengths corresponding to the numbers 1 to 9 which were used to enter numbers into the totalling device. One stepped drum for each position in the setting mechanism can be moved parallel to the axis via tooth racks. The set number determined by the length of the cogs is then gripped by a mating gear, entering it into the totalling device. Using this novel machine part presented a great advantage since all numbers could be entered into the totalling device simultaneously, thus enabling the machine to multiply or divide numbers. Inspired by clock technology in Paris, Leibniz devised the stepped drum in 1672. He presented the first wooden model for entering numbers via stepped drums to the Royal Society of London in 1673 and to the French Academy of Sciences in 1675.

## 提要

阶梯鼓（Staffelwalze）为莱布尼茨 1672 年在巴黎受钟表技术启发而发明；1673 年向伦敦皇家学会、1675 年向巴黎科学院演示木模型。阶梯鼓使全部数位可同时入总计装置，从而使乘除机械化。历代机器中仅存“小机器”一台（1690 年起造，1692 年完成，1716 年归汉诺威），今藏戈特弗里德·威廉·莱布尼茨图书馆。
