## 正文摘录（逐字）

### 块 15（批处理的“祭司/侍从”仪式与延迟）

> All these people in charge of punching cards, feeding them into readers, and pressing buttons and switches on the machine were what was commonly called a Priesthood, and those privileged enough to submit data to those most holy priests were the official acolytes.

> As a general rule, even these most privileged of acolytes were not allowed direct access to the machine itself, and they would not be able to see for hours, sometimes for days, the results of the machine's ingestion of their "batch" of cards.

### 块 94（Project MAC 分时项目的经费来源）

> So by the early sixties, MIT had obtained a long-range grant for its time-sharing project, which would be named Project MAC (the initials stood for two things: Multiple Access Computing, and Machine Aided Cognition). Uncle Sam would cough up three million dollars a year.

### 块 7（IBM 批处理的形象）

> IBM was The Enemy and this was its machine, the Hulking Giant computer in MIT's Building 26. Later modified into the IBM 709, then the IBM 7090. Batch-processed and intolerable.

## 提要

Levy 1984 年记者著述记早期计算文化：批处理时代操作计算机者被称为“祭司”，提交作业者须经其手，连特权用户也须等数小时乃至数日才见结果；1960 年代初 MIT 获长期经费建分时项目 Project MAC（Multiple Access Computing／Machine Aided Cognition），联邦每年约三百万美元；IBM 批处理机被黑客视为“庞大巨兽”“不可容忍”。均为二手叙述，作背景性材料，须与一手交叉验证。
